- Q1: MIT for all of sqll; `LICENSE`: "Copyright (c) 2026 The sqll authors". The ported base (sqlfluffrs) and the generated tables (sqlfluff) are MIT, and MIT permits the duties of all current dependencies.
- Q2: moot for option 2. If sqll ports sqruff code, those files keep Apache-2.0 with its full text and notices, plus the sqlfluff MIT notice.
- Q3: yes, the generated tables are a substantial portion. Each sqll binary carries the sqlfluff MIT copyright and permission notice (`sqll --license`).
- Q4: `crates/sqll/build.rs` makes the dependency notice list from cargo metadata at build time; `sqll --license` prints it. The build and a test fail closed on a license outside the allow list (THIRD_PARTY.md, "Rust").
- Q5: yes, `sqlfluffrs/LICENSE.md` covers each sqlfluffrs crate. Each ported crate has `license = "MIT"` and keeps its attribution header.
- Q6: yes, the notice list records each OR choice ("Chosen:").
- Q7: moot for option 2. If sqll ports sqruff code later, keep its appendix copyright line and the full license.

Open for release packaging (not in S3): the notices in `COPYRIGHT` of the Rust standard library, other than its MIT notice. `sqll --license` prints the MIT notice and names the file.
